Olive Branch: Family Conversation Cards

A free, browser-based conversation card game built for real families, not a one-time icebreaker. Hundreds of questions across seven categories, from light to deep, most written to be asked again months or years apart and land somewhere new each time. Works fully offline once loaded, nothing to install, nothing to buy.

Communication is the foundation everything else gets built on: leadership, public speaking, confidence. That foundation gets built at a table in ordinary conversation, years before anyone is thinking about leadership at all. That's what this game is for.

Heart Check: A 5-Week Feelings Workbook

A free, printable workbook that walks your child through five skills, one a week: noticing a feeling, calming a big one, noticing feelings in other people, saying a feeling out loud, and connecting a feeling to a choice. Every week pairs the skill with an authentic hadith or ayah, so the skill and the faith are taught as one thing, not two.

Each week ends with one small prompt for you, not a lecture, just one question at dinner or bedtime. Built for homeschooling, or just for use at your own kitchen table.

Explore Further

  • On the Prophet's emotional intelligence, peace be upon him: With the Heart in Mind by Shaykh Mikaeel Ahmed Smith, a study of the moral and emotional intelligence of the Prophet, peace be upon him, built on classical Islamic scholarship.
  • On faith and mental health together: the work of Dr. Rania Awaad at Stanford's Muslim Mental Health & Islamic Psychology Lab, bridging Islamic tradition and modern psychological care.
  • On an Islamic framework for therapy: Dr. Hooman Keshavarzi's Traditional Islamically Integrated Psychotherapy, a peer-reviewed model grounding modern psychotherapy in classical Islamic scholarship.
  • On naming your feelings: Permission to Feel by Marc Brackett, the Yale researcher behind RULER, the framework much of our emotional intelligence work draws from.
  • On leadership: "What Makes a Leader?" by Daniel Goleman in Harvard Business Review, the case for emotional intelligence mattering more than IQ.
  • On public speaking fear: the research of James McCroskey on communication apprehension, decades of work showing speaking anxiety is a skill gap, not a fixed trait.
  • On neurodivergent kids: recent peer-reviewed research on strengths-based, neurodiversity-affirming approaches to ADHD and autism in education.
  • On grit and character: Grit by Angela Duckworth, on why sustained effort predicts success as strongly as talent does.
