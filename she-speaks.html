About the Day

You were not born silent. That came later.

She Speaks: A Return to Fitrah is rooted in the Islamic concept of fitrah, the innate God-given nature every person is born with.

Somewhere along the way, most of us learned to make ourselves smaller. In rooms, in relationships, in our own minds. This is a full day of guided reflection, embodied voice practice, Islamic meditation, and honest conversation in a circle of women who understand exactly what you mean.

35 seats. One room. A return to the voice that was always yours.

Your Facilitators

Nisha Ravindranath

Salam Spaces

Nisha Ravindranath is the founder of Salam Spaces, a GTA-based community initiative dedicated to creating meaningful programs, events, and spaces for Muslim children and families. A homeschooling mother and revert to Islam, Nisha brings a lived understanding of what it means to build a faith-rooted life from the inside out. Her work is grounded in community, connection, and the belief that Muslim families deserve spaces where they can grow together with intention. At She Speaks, she brings that same intention to the circle, holding space for women to name what they have been carrying and to speak it aloud.

Rimshah Ahmed

Olive Tree Institute

Rimshah Ahmed is a TEDx Ontario Public Service speaker, award-winning Speaker Slam Top 3 North America finalist, and founder of Olive Tree Institute. A program designer, educator, and Muslim community leader, she has spoken at the Canadian Mental Health Association (CMHA) National Conference and advocates for faith-rooted mental health support for Muslim women, youth, and neurodivergent families. Her work builds the programs, curricula, and spaces where Muslim communities grow into their fullest selves.

Maryam Anwer

Taqwa Community

Maryam Anwer is an educator, speaker, and founder of The Taqwa Community, a faith-centered platform creating meaningful spaces for Muslim women to learn, reflect, and grow through their relationship with Allah(swt) and the Qur'an. She holds a certification from Adwam and is currently enrolled at Qalam Seminary, deepening her foundation for building communities where women come as they are, grow in faith, and discover the impact they are capable of making. Her work sits at the intersection of faith, personal development, and women's empowerment, grounded in the belief that women who are given space to be heard without judgment become more courageous in using their voices for meaningful change.

Frequently Asked Questions
What is She Speaks?

She Speaks is a full-day gathering for Muslim women rooted in the Islamic concept of fitrah. It is not a conference, a therapy session, or a lecture. It is an intentional day of guided reflection, voice practice, Islamic meditation, and honest conversation in a small circle of women who understand what you are carrying.

Who is this for?

Any Muslim woman who has felt herself go quiet in rooms she deserved to take up space in. Whether you are confident on the outside but silent on the inside, or you know exactly what you want to say but something holds you back, this day was built for you.

Do I need to be a public speaker or comfortable speaking in front of people?

No. This is not a public speaking event. You will not be put on the spot or asked to perform. The speaking we do is honest and intimate, in a small circle where every woman is in the same place you are.

Is this a therapy session?

No. She Speaks is not therapy and is not a substitute for mental health support. It is a faith-rooted personal development experience. If you are currently working through something heavy, it may complement your journey but it is not clinical care.

Is this only for practicing Muslims?

The day is rooted in Islamic concepts and will include salah, dua, and Quranic references. It is designed for Muslim women who want a faith-integrated experience, not a secular wellness event.

What does the day actually look like?

You arrive to breakfast and registration, then move through facilitated sessions on voice, Islamic meditation, the stories of Muslim women before us, personal truth-telling, and dua writing. There is a sound bath, a nourishing lunch, and salah built into the day. You leave by 5:00 PM.

Will I have to share personal things with the group?

You are never required to share anything. The space invites honesty but never demands it. You can participate fully by simply being present and listening.

How many women will be there?

35 women. This is intentionally small so the space stays intimate, safe, and unhurried.

What is included in the ticket price?

Welcome breakfast, refreshments throughout the day, a nourishing lunch, all sessions, and a gift for each attendee.

What should I wear?

Comfortable, modest clothing. You may be sitting on the floor for some sessions, so dress accordingly.

Is there parking at the venue?

My Vitae is located at 1155 North Service Road West, Oakville. Parking is available at the venue.

Can I get a refund if I cannot attend?

Tickets are non-refundable. However, you may transfer your ticket to another person.

Can I bring a friend?

Yes, and we encourage it. Buy your tickets separately and let us know in the intake form so we can seat you together.
